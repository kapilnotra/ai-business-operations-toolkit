# AI Opportunity Assessment Framework

## Purpose

A practical framework for business leaders to evaluate whether an AI opportunity is sufficiently valuable, practical, responsible and measurable to progress into portfolio prioritization.

The objective is not to identify the largest possible number of AI use cases.

The objective is to identify opportunities where AI can create **measurable business value** and where there is a credible path to implementation and value realization.

---

## 1. Define the Business Problem

Start with the **business problem rather than the technology**.

### Key questions

* What problem are we trying to solve?
* Who experiences the problem?
* How significant is the problem?
* How is it currently being managed?
* What is the cost of the current approach?
* What are the limitations of the current process or solution?
* What would success look like?
* What measurable business outcome are we trying to achieve?

### Principle

> **Start with the outcome, not the AI technology.**

---

## 2. Identify the AI Opportunity

Consider whether AI could materially improve the business problem through capabilities such as:

* Automating repetitive work
* Improving decision-making
* Accelerating analysis
* Generating or transforming content
* Improving customer or employee experiences
* Predicting outcomes
* Identifying patterns
* Supporting knowledge workers
* Reducing operational risk
* Improving productivity

The objective is not to force AI into a process.

The question is:

> **Could AI create a materially better business outcome than the current approach or viable alternatives?**

---

## 3. Assess Business Value

Evaluate the potential business value across multiple dimensions.

| Dimension       | Key Question                                                                     |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Revenue         | Could this increase revenue, growth or retention?                                |
| Cost            | Could this reduce cost or improve productivity?                                  |
| Risk            | Could this reduce operational, financial or business risk?                       |
| Experience      | Could this improve customer or employee experience?                              |
| Strategic Value | Could this create or strengthen a strategic capability or competitive advantage? |

### Business Value assessment

**1 = Very Low**

**5 = Very High**

The assessment should consider both the **magnitude of the potential outcome** and the number of business dimensions affected.

---

## 4. Assess Feasibility

An attractive opportunity is not necessarily a practical one.

Assess:

* Data availability
* Data quality
* Technology readiness
* Process maturity
* Integration requirements
* Skills and capabilities
* Operating model
* Change requirements
* Implementation complexity
* Organizational readiness

### Feasibility assessment

**1 = Very Difficult**

**5 = Very Easy**

A higher score represents a more achievable opportunity using available or realistically obtainable capabilities.

---

## 5. Assess Time to Value

Estimate how quickly measurable business benefits could realistically be achieved.

| Score | Indicative Time to Value |
| ----: | ------------------------ |
| **1** | More than 24 months      |
| **2** | 12–24 months             |
| **3** | 6–12 months              |
| **4** | 3–6 months               |
| **5** | Less than 3 months       |

These ranges are indicative and may be adapted for the organization's circumstances.

---

## 6. Assess Risk and Responsible Implementation

Consider the risks associated with implementing and operating the AI capability.

Areas to consider include:

* Data privacy
* Cybersecurity
* Accuracy
* Bias
* Regulatory requirements
* Intellectual property
* Model reliability
* Human oversight
* Reputational impact
* Operational resilience

### Risk Manageability assessment

**1 = Very Difficult to Manage**

**5 = Very Easy to Manage**

A higher score represents a more manageable risk profile.

The assessment should consider both the **inherent risk** and the organization's ability to establish appropriate controls.

---

## 7. Assess Strategic Alignment

Evaluate how closely the opportunity supports the organization's strategic priorities.

### Strategic Alignment

**1 = Very Low**

**5 = Very High**

Consider whether the opportunity:

* Supports a stated strategic priority
* Advances a transformation agenda
* Strengthens a critical capability
* Supports competitive positioning
* Enables an important future-state operating model

---

## 8. Assess Probability of Success

Estimate the likelihood that the opportunity can achieve its intended business outcome.

Consider:

* Strength of the business case
* Availability and quality of data
* Technical feasibility
* Organizational readiness
* Leadership sponsorship
* Change complexity
* User adoption
* Ability to measure the outcome
* Evidence from comparable implementations or experiments

| Score | Indicative Probability |
| ----: | ---------------------- |
| **1** | Less than 20%          |
| **2** | 20–40%                 |
| **3** | 40–60%                 |
| **4** | 60–80%                 |
| **5** | Greater than 80%       |

This dimension deliberately distinguishes:

> **Potential value**

from

> **Likelihood of successfully realizing that value.**

---

## 9. Define the Minimum Viable Use Case

Before investing in a large AI transformation, identify the smallest practical version of the opportunity that can demonstrate value.

Define:

| Element          | Definition                                   |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| User             | Who will use or benefit from the capability? |
| Problem          | What specific problem will be addressed?     |
| AI Capability    | What will the AI actually do?                |
| Required Data    | What data is required?                       |
| Expected Outcome | What business outcome is expected?           |
| Success Metric   | How will success be measured?                |
| Risk Controls    | What controls are required?                  |
| Human Oversight  | Where is human judgment required?            |

The minimum viable use case should be sufficiently narrow to allow the organization to generate evidence before making a larger investment.

---

## 10. Define Success Measures

Every AI opportunity should have measurable outcomes.

Examples include:

* Hours saved
* Cost reduction
* Cycle-time reduction
* Revenue generated
* Error reduction
* Customer satisfaction
* Employee experience
* Adoption
* Quality improvement
* Risk reduction

The objective is to move from:

> **"We implemented AI."**

to:

> **"AI created measurable business value."**

---

## 11. Assessment Output

The assessment should produce a structured **AI Opportunity Profile** containing:

* Business problem
* Proposed AI opportunity
* Business value assessment
* Feasibility assessment
* Time to value
* Risk manageability
* Strategic alignment
* Probability of success
* Minimum viable use case
* Success measures
* Key assumptions
* Key uncertainties

The output then feeds the **AI Use-Case Prioritization Framework**.

---

## 12. Relationship to Prioritization

The Opportunity Assessment determines whether an opportunity is sufficiently understood and credible to enter portfolio prioritization.

It does **not** determine the final investment decision.

The broader decision journey is:

**Business Problem → Opportunity Assessment → Portfolio Prioritization → Business Case → Investment Decision → Execution → Value Realization**

Portfolio prioritization considers the assessed dimensions alongside competing opportunities.

Financial analysis is performed separately through the **AI Business Case Framework**.

---

## 13. Guiding Principle

An AI opportunity should not progress simply because the technology is interesting or technically impressive.

A strong opportunity combines:

**Meaningful Business Value**

*

**Practical Feasibility**

*

**Reasonable Time to Value**

*

**Manageable Risk**

*

**Strategic Alignment**

*

**Credible Probability of Success**

*

**Measurable Outcomes**

---

## Conclusion

AI opportunities should be managed as **business opportunities first and technology initiatives second**.

The assessment framework provides a disciplined way to move from:

**Business Problem → AI Opportunity → Evidence → Prioritization**

while maintaining focus on measurable business outcomes, responsible implementation and the probability of successful value realization.

The framework is independently developed for professional portfolio purposes. All examples and scoring ranges are illustrative.
