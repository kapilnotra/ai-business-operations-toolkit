# AI Use-Case Prioritization Matrix

## Purpose

A practical decision-making tool for business leaders evaluating multiple potential AI opportunities.

The purpose is to focus investment and leadership attention on AI opportunities that offer meaningful business value while remaining feasible and appropriately governed.

---

## 1. Evaluation Dimensions

Each AI use case is assessed across five dimensions.

### Business Value

How significant could the potential business benefit be?

**1 = Very Low**

**5 = Very High**

Consider:

* Revenue opportunity
* Cost reduction
* Productivity
* Customer experience
* Employee experience
* Risk reduction
* Strategic importance

---

### Feasibility

How practical is implementation?

**1 = Very Difficult**

**5 = Very Easy**

Consider:

* Data availability
* Technology readiness
* Integration requirements
* Skills
* Process maturity
* Organizational readiness

---

### Time to Value

How quickly could measurable benefits be achieved?

**1 = More than 18 months**

**2 = 12–18 months**

**3 = 6–12 months**

**4 = 3–6 months**

**5 = Less than 3 months**

---

### Risk

What level of business, regulatory, security, privacy or reputational risk exists?

**1 = Very High Risk**

**5 = Very Low Risk**

A higher score represents a lower level of risk.

Consider:

* Data privacy
* Cybersecurity
* Regulatory requirements
* Accuracy
* Bias
* Intellectual property
* Human oversight
* Reputational impact

---

### Strategic Alignment

How closely does the opportunity support strategic business priorities?

**1 = Very Low**

**5 = Very High**

---

## 2. Recommended Scoring Model

A simple weighted score can be used:

> **Priority Score = (Business Value × 30%) + (Feasibility × 20%) + (Time to Value × 20%) + (Risk × 15%) + (Strategic Alignment × 15%)**

The weighting should be adapted to the organization's specific circumstances.

This is a portfolio decision-support tool rather than a financial valuation methodology.

## 4. Decision Categories

Use the score to place opportunities into four broad categories.

### Prioritize

High value, feasible and appropriately controlled.

**Recommended action:** Begin detailed discovery or pilot planning.

### Experiment

Potentially valuable but requiring further validation.

**Recommended action:** Conduct a small proof of concept.

### Investigate

Potentially significant opportunity but with substantial complexity or risk.

**Recommended action:** Conduct further analysis before committing significant resources.

### Defer

Low value, low feasibility or insufficient strategic alignment.

**Recommended action:** Do not prioritize at this stage.

---

## 5. Executive Decision Questions

Before approving an AI initiative, leadership should ask:

1. What business problem are we solving?
2. What measurable outcome are we targeting?
3. Why is AI the appropriate solution?
4. What alternatives have been considered?
5. What data is required?
6. What are the major risks?
7. Who owns the outcome?
8. How will human oversight work?
9. What is the expected time to value?
10. What evidence would justify scaling?

---

## 6. Recommended Governance Principle

An AI opportunity should not be prioritized solely because it is technically impressive.

The strongest opportunities combine:

**High Business Value**

*

**Practical Feasibility**

*

**Fast or Reasonable Time to Value**

*

**Manageable Risk**

*

**Strategic Alignment**

---

## Conclusion

AI portfolios should be managed as business investments rather than collections of technology experiments.

The objective is to create a disciplined process for moving from:

**Ideas → Prioritization → Experimentation → Evidence → Investment → Scale**

This framework is independently developed for professional portfolio purposes. All examples and scores are illustrative.
